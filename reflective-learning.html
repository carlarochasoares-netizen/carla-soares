Why Reflection Matters

Reflection has helped me connect my architectural background with construction project management. By evaluating experiences, feedback and challenges, I can turn academic learning into practical actions that support my career in New Zealand's residential construction industry.

02 / LEARNING

Key Learning Experiences

How academic study, teamwork and feedback have influenced my professional mindset.

01

Learning to See Projects Beyond Design

Coming from an architectural background, I naturally focused on creating functional and aesthetically pleasing spaces. Through the Project Management course, I learned that even the best design depends on effective planning, realistic scheduling, clear communication and proactive risk management.

The residential redevelopment case study shifted my thinking from only asking what should be built to considering how the project would be delivered successfully. Scope, time, cost, quality and stakeholder management became part of one integrated picture.

Architecture and project management are complementary disciplines. Combining design knowledge with structured planning will help me contribute more effectively to residential projects in New Zealand.
02

The Value of Teamwork in a Multicultural Environment

Most of my previous experience was in Brazilian workplaces, where communication styles and expectations were familiar. Working with classmates from varied cultural and professional backgrounds at ICL introduced new ways of approaching problems.

I learned to listen carefully, respect alternative viewpoints, ask clarifying questions and adapt my communication style. Differences that initially felt challenging often produced richer discussions and more creative solutions.

Successful multicultural teamwork is built on openness, mutual respect and a willingness to learn from others.
03

Building Confidence Through Active Class Participation

Public speaking, particularly in English, was an area I wanted to improve. Classroom discussions, group activities and opportunities to answer questions gradually helped me become more confident.

I came to understand that professional communication is not limited to formal presentations. It also involves explaining reasoning clearly, listening to different perspectives and contributing meaningfully to discussion.

Confidence develops through consistent practice. I will continue to speak up, ask questions and participate actively as I progress into Construction Project Management.
04

Learning Through Continuous Improvement

Constructive feedback from lecturers taught me to see revision as an essential part of professional growth rather than as criticism. Refining assignments and reports strengthened my analytical thinking, attention to detail and ability to communicate ideas.

I now approach challenges with a more reflective mindset: evaluate, adapt and persist. High-quality work is often an iterative process shaped by collaboration and feedback.

Continuous improvement is not about avoiding mistakes; it is about learning from them and making thoughtful changes over time.
05

Building a New Career in New Zealand

Moving to New Zealand for postgraduate study required resilience, flexibility and a willingness to keep learning. Adapting to a new education system, communicating in English and understanding a different professional environment pushed me beyond what was familiar.

Construction Project Management has confirmed my professional direction by connecting my architectural experience with new management knowledge and a broader understanding of residential project delivery.

Building a career in a new country takes time, but adaptability, resilience and lifelong learning will help me contribute to multidisciplinary teams and New Zealand's residential construction industry.
03 / FEEDBACK

Turning Feedback into Action

Receiving this feedback was encouraging and insightful. I was pleased that my teammate recognised organisation, responsibility and communication — values I strive to demonstrate in both academic and professional settings.

I also recognise that my strong sense of responsibility can lead me to take on too much work myself. Developing confidence in delegation will help me become a more effective project manager.
